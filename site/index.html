Stock trading basics

The foundation before you touch options.

Getting started

  1. Open a brokerage account (taxable, IRA or Roth IRA).
  2. Fund it by bank transfer.
  3. Search a ticker, review price, volume and fundamentals.
  4. Place an order and confirm the details.
  5. Track, rebalance and keep records for taxes.

Order types

  • Market: buys now at the best available price. Fast, but the price can slip.
  • Limit: buys or sells only at your price or better. Preferred for most trades.
  • Stop / stop-limit: triggers when price hits a level, to cap losses.
  • Day vs GTC: expires today, or stays open until canceled.

Core principles

  • Diversify across companies, sectors and asset types.
  • Broad index ETFs are a low-cost core holding.
  • Size positions so one loss cannot hurt you badly.
  • Dividend yield is only one part of total return.
  • Only invest money you will not need soon.

Options trading basics

An option is a contract on 100 shares of a stock.

Key terms

  • Call: right to buy at the strike price. Put: right to sell.
  • Strike: the agreed price. Expiration: the last day the contract is valid.
  • Premium: the option's price per share (x100 per contract).
  • In / at / out of the money: strike below, near or above the stock price (for calls).
  • Delta: sensitivity to the stock price, and a rough chance of finishing in the money.

How to place an options trade

  1. Apply for options approval with your broker.
  2. Open the option chain for a ticker.
  3. Pick expiration, then strike.
  4. Choose the action: Buy to Open, Sell to Open, Buy to Close or Sell to Close.
  5. Use a limit order near the mid price between bid and ask.

Risks to respect

  • Bought options can expire worthless: you can lose 100% of the premium.
  • Time decay (theta) works against buyers and for sellers.
  • Selling uncovered options can mean large losses. Beginners should stay with defined-risk or covered strategies.
  • Wide bid/ask spreads on thin options quietly cost money.

Covered calls, step by step

Own 100 shares, sell a call against them, collect the premium.

How it works

  1. Own at least 100 shares of a stock you are willing to sell.
  2. Sell to Open one call per 100 shares, usually out of the money.
  3. Collect the premium immediately.
  4. At expiration, one of three things happens.

Example: You own 100 shares of XYZ at $50. Sell a 1-month $53 call for $0.90. You receive $90.

Possible outcomes

  • Stock stays below $53: the call expires worthless. You keep the shares and the $90. Repeat.
  • Stock rises above $53: shares are called away at $53. Maximum gain is $300 stock gain plus $90 premium = $390.
  • Stock falls: you keep the premium, which cushions the loss. Breakeven is $49.10, but the shares can still lose value.

Trade-offs and checks

  • Upside is capped at the strike plus premium.
  • Check earnings and ex-dividend dates before expiry. Early assignment risk rises before ex-dividend dates.
  • Lower strikes pay more but are more likely to be assigned.
  • Rolling: buy back the call and sell a later or higher one.
  • Assignment can trigger taxable gains. Check tax rules for your account.

Spreading capital for income

Edit the amount, allocations and yields to estimate cash income from dividends and covered calls.

Example: $100,000 income mix

  • 40% broad index ETFs (growth, low cost)
  • 25% dividend growth stocks
  • 15% higher-yield income ETFs
  • 20% T-bills or cash (stability and opportunities)

Only write calls on stocks you are happy to sell, and keep any single stock to a size you can afford to lose.

Illustrative only. Dividends can be cut, prices fall, and premium varies with volatility. Allocation must total 100%.
